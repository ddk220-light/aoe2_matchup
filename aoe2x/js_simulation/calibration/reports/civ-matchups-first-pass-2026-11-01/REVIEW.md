# Prediction-first completion pass

The owner now accepts explicitly flagged, unvalidated guesses in the first-pass table and wants simulations concentrated on a small validation sample and the remaining hard gaps. These files implement that revised goal. They do not claim a fully validated dataset or change the web app.

Against the reviewed phase-026 matrix, the first-pass policy supplies **39,375 predictions out of 52,374 unresolved profile pairs (75.18%)**, expanding to **54,773 civilization cells**. **12,999 profile pairs remain for direct resolution**, including 13 blocked pairs. The full export retains all 68,916 profile pairs, all 151,223 displayed cells and the complete 555-variant roster. Source outcomes remain available separately from the provisional outcomes. Every guessed row is labelled `predicted_unvalidated`, `verified: false`, and `needsValidation: true`.

The policy retains the stronger existing candidates, and adds pairs where two frozen models agree, their mean winner score is at least 0.775, each score is at least 0.60, and neither close-fight score exceeds 0.15. These are model-selection scores, not calibrated win probabilities. The models use ranking HP margins, common-opponent outcomes and margins, damage/armor classes, army counts and unit mechanics. There was no new model training or simulation to generate this export. Old-engine rankings remain predictive features only.

The phase-026 prospective check of the earlier 28,013-pair pool found 129 correct predictions among 130 completed candidates, one close fight incorrectly called decisive, and three candidates with missing seeds. The close fight was 21 Spanish Heavy Mounted Crossbowmen versus 27 Berber Hussars: ten seeds split 5–5. Ranged benchmarks favored the Hussars, while shared melee benchmarks favored the crossbowmen. The revised two-benchmark cavalry rule requires confirming shared melee margins. It removes only that method's unsupported call; other methods and other subtypes remain eligible.

Screening the broader first-pass policy against phases 025–026 selected 362 completed fights: **347 correct, 11 wrong winners, and 4 close fights called decisive**. The policy was chosen after inspecting those phases, so this is a screening result, not independent validation. All mistakes, contexts, source-run locations and common-C features are retained in `026/validation.json`. The frozen policy is introduced after phase 027; phase 028 onward supplies independent tests. No predicted row is reused as a training label or independent anchor.

Each future 256-pair phase reserves **32 predictions for validation**. Half are deterministic representative samples and half emphasize decision boundaries and unfamiliar profiles. Sampling favors precise subtype/effect/armor/scenario strata that cover many cells relative to estimated processing cost, while reducing priority as they receive tests. Remaining slots use the existing refinement and hard-case queues after excluding deferred predictions. A newly failed precise stratum is recorded and returned to direct testing pending a narrower rule revision; an entire unit family is not discarded.

The selector writes a versioned table, summary, validation ledger, failures and selection snapshot after each reviewed phase. It sends `deferredPredictionKeys` separately from `skippedVerifiedEstimateKeys` to the runner. Earlier V3-only estimates are no longer mislabeled as compatible V2-verified skips. Frozen combat source files, scenarios, counts, seed policy and all historical journals remain unchanged. V2 collision repair, ranking anchors and streaming-hash recovery continue separately; these first-pass predictions are based on frozen V3 and do not establish V2 compatibility.

The implementation tests cover model disagreement, close risk, narrow benchmark revision, exclusion of inferred labels, independent-phase boundaries, deterministic bounded samples, preservation of observed and blocked rows, display-direction inversion, and immutable source tables. The reviewed-phase gate also rejects incomplete or mismatched source snapshots.
