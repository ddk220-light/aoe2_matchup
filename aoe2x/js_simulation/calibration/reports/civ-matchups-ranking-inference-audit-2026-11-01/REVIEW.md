# Ranking evidence as a civ-matchup predictor (offline phase-019 audit)

This audit uses the immutable phase-019 V3 checkpoint. It ran no fights and did not change the live runner. Ranking records from the original engine are **predictive features only**, never current-engine measurements. The corrected ranking anchors remain eligible direct roots under the frozen source checks.

The unit-ranking table has an individual result against each relevant standard opponent. A profile's fingerprint is its signed winner-HP fraction against each such standard, averaged across all saved seeds. The audit compares two profiles against the **same** standards; it never interprets the overall ranking score as proof of a head-to-head winner.

## What the saved fights show

- Of 1,645 original-engine ranking matchups overlapping a completed V3 civ fight, 1,578 kept the same winner, 39 flipped, and 28 were mixed or close. All 707 with a minimum winner-HP fraction of at least 50% kept the winner. This does not make the old result a V3 observation.
- A global score ordering got 926/1,275 later-phase observed pairs right. A common-standard mean margin gap of at least 0.5 got 204/210 right. Neither is safe to publish as a decisive conclusion.
- Requiring at least three common standards and a margin advantage of at least 0.5 against **each** got 178/180 across all observed pairs and 33/33 in phases 015-019. The failures were Composite Bowman versus Scorpion and Heavy Cavalry Archer versus Skirmisher: target-specific counters invalidated the general benchmark ordering.
- Restricting the three-standard unanimous rule to matchup families with at least ten error-free early-phase examples enabled four families at gap 0.5 and twelve at gap 0.25. The respective later-phase holdouts were 19/19 and 64/64. The latter yields 3,907 unresolved profile-pair **candidates**, not 3,907 verified winners. These families require compatible V2 replay validation and explicit effect checks before use as final estimates.
- A seed-by-seed interval rule at a 0.5 gap still missed one of 151 all-phase observed cases, a Skirmisher versus Heavy Cavalry Archer counter. Larger statistical margins alone do not remove type interaction.
- The granular common-C search considered 48 unit-line pairs with at least 25 observed fights. For each five-fold split, it chose a C subset and minimum margin gap using four folds, then tested the fifth. Fourteen line pairs had at least 20 cross-validated predictions and no errors, totaling 725 predictions. Their fitted rules suggest 8,339 still-unresolved profile-pair **estimates**; 4,898 of those involve effects the static inference rules do not yet understand. These are estimates with selection and engine-version limits, not 8,339 validated winners.
- Archer-versus-archer illustrates why C selection matters. Saracen Hand Cannoneer at a 0.1 signed HP-margin gap separated 29/29 verified archer pairs in pair-level cross-validation and identifies 389 unresolved candidates. Chinese Arbalester at that gap separated 11/11. Requiring Hand Cannoneer plus Champion selected 22/23 correctly in cross-validation; more Cs alone did not improve reliability. There are only 46 observed archer mirror profiles and just two from phases 015-019, so this is a promising rule, not a proven universal ordering.
- Other same-line results are mixed: Scorpion-versus-Scorpion selected Chinese Arbalester plus Champion (26/26), whereas Knight-versus-Knight selected Heavy Cavalry Archer plus Champion (91/97), Cavalry Archer-versus-Cavalry Archer 22/24, Militia-versus-Militia 17/18, and Skirmisher-versus-Skirmisher 7/8. The latter four cannot become decisive rules as fitted.
- Ignoring cost while retaining every other mechanics field and requiring identical integer army counts, scenario, and orientation yields 108 currently unresolved pairs with an observed physical-equivalence anchor. Two later-phase held-out pairs were both correct. This needs a formal runtime-field audit before final equivalence use.
- A separate narrowly scoped inactive recipient-group rule added 1,302 inferred profile pairs versus the previous v4 inference build (57,428 to 56,126 unresolved). Its R1 held-out check made 2,636 correct predictions and zero wrong predictions across 1,003 target pairs. Paladin mirrors were explicitly excluded after a broader version produced counterexamples. R2/R3 do not use the new group rule. The full matrix validator passed: 68,916 profile pairs, 151,223 cells, zero simulations.

The most useful next inference rule is an opponent-conditioned bridge: A and B must separate decisively against the same C **and** C's attack, armor, bonus classes, range, mobility, army counts, and active effects must make it a valid stand-in for the proposed A-versus-B fight. The benchmark margin is supporting evidence, not the transfer proof. A family rule should be trained on earlier profile pairs, tested on later disjoint pairs and then repeated under the consistent repaired engine before upgrading estimates to final conclusions. Close and conflicting cases abstain.
