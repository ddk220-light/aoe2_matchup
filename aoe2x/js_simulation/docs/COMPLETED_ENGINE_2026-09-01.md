# Completed JavaScript combat engine baseline — 2026-09-01

## Status

This document freezes the current JavaScript simulator as the completed
mechanics baseline for the next roster-expansion phase. The baseline is not a
claim that every Age of Empires II subsystem is implemented. It means the
golden-scenario combat engine, viewer, current registered units, and reusable
mechanics described below are the reference implementation from which new unit
families will be added.

The engine is built from scratch against project-local live `frames.bin`
captures. It does not load game-engine code. Unit attributes come from the
generated mechanics fixtures; live captures validate behavior and outcomes.

The governing invariant is reusable mechanics, never output fitting. A live
winner, survivor HP, timestamp, waypoint, or later target assignment may not
be inserted as a runtime correction. Scenario-authored rosters, positions,
orders, diplomacy, and triggers are inputs and may be reproduced exactly. The
only stochastic calibration boundary is first acquisition: its initial target
and pre-acquisition delay may be selected by a generic seeded policy. After
first acquisition, movement, retargeting, firing, and damage are generated by
the engine.

## Execution model

Combat advances at 60 deterministic ticks per second. A world contains the
map, live units, diplomacy, scenario triggers, active orders, target and route
state, attacks, projectiles, reservations, event log, and seeded opening state.
For a fixed scenario and opening seed, the result and event order are stable.

Each tick performs these conceptual stages:

1. Apply due scenario trigger effects, including patrol orders and diplomacy
   changes.
2. Validate living targets and cancel attacks whose target or diplomacy is no
   longer valid.
3. Acquire or retarget from current visibility, reachability, target pressure,
   and the unit's active order.
4. Propose movement from speed, acceleration, minimum-range retreat, pursuit,
   patrol, and route state.
5. Resolve local avoidance, body collision, allied overlap, ranged crowding,
   contact reservations, and map bounds.
6. Advance attack wind-up and reload clocks; release melee hits or ranged
   projectiles when ready.
7. Move projectiles and resolve interception, misses, pass-through, blast,
   debris, trample, friendly fire, damage, and deaths.
8. Apply defeat-dependent trigger effects, clear invalid state, evaluate the
   scenario victory teams, and emit the immutable next world state.

`src/fight.js` builds a battle from registry fixtures and scenario inputs.
`src/combat/world.js` owns the tick pipeline. Smaller modules own targeting,
damage calculations, avoidance, route planning, ranged crowding, contact
reservations, and projectile geometry. `server.mjs` exposes the viewer and
fight endpoints without introducing a second combat implementation.

## Scenario and diplomacy layer

The engine supports the four golden arrangements: melee-versus-melee,
ranged-versus-ranged, ranged-versus-melee, and melee-versus-ranged.

- Golden unit slots are filled edge-to-centre using the scenario's stable unit
  order and the requested equal-resource counts, capped at 27 on the cheaper
  side.
- Trigger-authored patrol is a move toward the opposite endpoint until a unit
  acquires a valid target. It then stops ordinary patrol transit and attacks.
- Mixed ranged/melee scenarios preserve Player 4 as an independently colored
  auxiliary screen on the ranged side.
- The melee and ranged combat sides begin allied where the scenario says so.
  When Player 4 is defeated, the authored diplomacy effect makes the combat
  sides enemies and the later patrol/acquisition proceeds from that state.
- Victory is evaluated by scenario teams, not by assuming that numeric player
  IDs are always hostile.

No fixed Player-4 death time or delayed release timer is used. The release is
caused by actual damage, defeat, and the trigger/diplomacy transition.

## Movement, collision, and overlap

Unit motion uses mechanics-derived speed and acceleration. Collision uses the
physical collision footprint; the outline/selection footprint is exported to
the viewer but does not block movement or determine attack reach.

The movement solver combines short-range avoidance with persistent route
intent. A blocked pursuer searches body-safe tangent and grid routes around
the obstructing formation. Route commitment prevents a unit from reversing
its local choice every tick. A newly available target may be acquired while
the unit is routing; otherwise it continues toward a reachable firing or
contact position.

Allied overlap is stateful and bounded:

- Moving ranged units may compress into two- and limited three-body stacks.
- A fourth-body overlap is rejected.
- Deeper overlap has a much larger geometric cost than a pair overlap.
- Existing legal overlap can be inherited when units stop; it is not expanded
  indefinitely by later arrivals.
- A ranged Siege Weapon-class body that has committed to attacking or reloading
  becomes a path obstacle to new ingress. This is class-based and therefore
  applies to siege units generally, not to a named matchup.
- One-range melee contact uses the generic reach-wedge/contact-reservation
  rules. Zero-range melee keeps ordinary physical contact behavior.

These rules let firing lines form without either rigid grid locking or mass
phasing through the entire formation.

## Targeting and attack state

PATROL acquisition uses shared player vision and current contact geometry.
The seeded opening policy may vary the initial visible contact lane and the
delay before first acquisition. It cannot prescribe later combat.

After acquisition, target validity is generic across owners. Ranged units may
switch when a target dies, becomes invalid through diplomacy, is unreachable,
or a current-geometry opportunity/recovery scan finds a viable target. The
policy is not restricted to Player 2 or to a named unit matchup. Attack wind-up
and reload state are validated independently so a stale target cannot continue
receiving damage.

Melee attacks use body-edge reach, wind-up, reload, armor classes, minimum-one
damage, and death cancellation. Ranged attacks create projectiles; damage is
not applied at release time.

## Projectile and special-damage mechanics

The projectile system models launch-time aim, travel time, projectile width,
landing collision, and independently seeded shot accuracy.

- Hand Cannoneer accuracy comes from the unit fixture. A failed accuracy roll
  scatters the landing point; an unintended body at that point receives the
  game's reduced half post-armor damage.
- Scorpion bolts can hit bodies along their traveled segment. The first hit is
  full damage and later pass-through hits use the mechanics-derived fraction.
  A committed Siege Weapon firing line also obstructs fresh allied ingress.
- Elephant trample is centered on the attacker when the melee hit lands. Every
  hostile body intersecting the sourced blast circle receives the configured
  post-armor damage fraction.
- Onager-family shells retain their launch-time ground aim and do not gain
  ballistics when the target moves. The primary impact applies body-edge blast
  falloff and the one-damage floor. Splash can damage allied Player-4 units.
  Secondary debris uses deterministic equal-area landing points and normal
  landing collision.

Unit-line behavior is registered through `behaviorFamily`. Siege Onager uses
the `onager` family; future Rocket Cart/Onager-line units must register the same
family rather than adding matchup-specific branches.

## Onager-family movement state machine

The final accepted Onager model is intentionally small:

1. Follow the scenario PATROL forward.
2. Scan for enemies according to shared visibility and the seeded first-scan
   policy.
3. On first acquisition, stop patrol transit and fire if the selected target
   is in the legal range band.
4. If blocked while approaching, use the same persistent obstacle routing as
   other units.
5. If the selected target enters minimum range, try a legal backward or
   side-backward step.
6. If no retreat heading is available, select another currently visible target
   outside minimum range. If none exists, remain unable to fire until geometry
   changes.

The family changes decision behavior only. It does not inject a survivor HP,
winner, fixed route, or later engagement timestamp.

## Running and verification

From the repository root:

```powershell
node aoe2x/js_simulation/server.mjs --host 127.0.0.1 --port 5011
```

Focused mechanics verification:


reproducing historical probes, not supported production settings.

## Extension checklist

For a new unit, export a fully upgraded mechanics fixture, register its civ,
cost, class, and optional reusable behavior family, then add mechanic-level
tests before comparing outcomes. Decode the newest relevant live capture and
record its exact `frames.bin` path and run number in the derived report. If a
delta cannot be explained by a reusable mechanic, leave it unresolved rather
than adding a unit- or matchup-specific runtime rule.
