## No-force-fit rule

Replay data validates and diagnoses the engine; it does not prescribe a
matchup result. Continuous behavior must come from reusable mechanics such as
DAT speed, collision/overlap, LOS, reach, path obstruction, target state,
reload, projectile travel, damage, diplomacy, and trigger processing.

The engine must not use per-matchup release delays, winner/HP corrections,
forced later targets, copied replay waypoints, or outcome overrides. The only
permitted custom boundary is first acquisition: its first target and opening
scan time may be generated by a generic seeded policy because those vary at
the game-AI boundary. After first acquisition, continuous behavior is entirely
engine-driven.

## Implemented scenario mechanics

- Diplomacy is directional: one owner can treat another as allied while the
  reverse relation is enemy.
- Player 4 is a live auxiliary army of nine Spanish Imperial Scouts at the
  exact authored positions. Scouts have sourced unit stats, physical collision,
  targeting, damage, death, and victory-team behavior.
- The engine detects an owner defeat from world state. When the last Player-4
  unit dies, the exact scenario trigger changes the relevant diplomacy edge
  and issues the principal melee army's patrol.
- Ranged-vs-melee gates Player 3 melee behind Player 4; melee-vs-ranged gates
  Player 2 melee behind Player 4. The same trigger engine handles both.
- Group PATROL preserves authored formation offsets while moving at DAT speed.
  Units leave formation transit on acquisition and then use ordinary pursuit,
  obstruction, engagement, attack, and retarget mechanics.
- A non-outnumbered cohort concentrates its first acquisition on one anchor;
  an outnumbered principal cohort fans across roughly one anchor per four
  hostile units. P4 stays one committed auxiliary group. This first-target
  policy ends at acquisition and never prescribes later retargeting.
- During the auxiliary engagement, a physical enemy body that blocks a locked
  patrol route can capture the engagement after sustained obstruction. Once
  Player 4 is gone, the principal armies use normal target selection.

## Replay observations

The existing ranged-vs-melee Arbalester/Paladin set contains five full-rate
`frames.bin` streams. All five start with 27 Chinese Arbalesters, 14 Spanish
Paladins, and 9 Player-4 Scouts. Player 4 is defeated at 18.936–20.994 raw game
seconds (mean 19.866). Paladins win all five with HP
`879, 852, 819, 942, 744` (mean 847.2). Principal-army elimination occurs at

The existing opposite-orientation spike starts with 14 Spanish Paladins,
27 Chinese Arbalesters, and 9 Player-4 Scouts. Player 4 is defeated at about
20.96 raw game seconds; Paladins win with 924 HP, and the last Arbalester dies
at 51.72 raw game seconds. This direction needs five fresh runs before its
variance is considered measured.

The short `elimination_time_s` in capture manifests is the 1.7× replay/video
clock. Engine seconds must be compared with raw gRPC game seconds, not that
accelerated wall/video clock.

## Formation investigation (not enabled)

The frame diagnostics show that the current rigid group translation is not a
faithful model of the game's movement order. In the live Arbalester/Hand
Cannoneer opening at three raw seconds, the P2 formation has mean displacement
`(-0.621, +0.648)` with mean travel `1.267` tiles, while P3 has
`(+0.905, -0.661)` with mean travel `1.624`. The stable engine translates the
same formations much more rigidly: P2 `(-1.173, +1.041)`, mean `1.569`, and P3
`(+1.234, -1.304)`, mean `1.796`. A fitted diagnostic affine map also shows
roughly 0.52–0.62 scale compression in the live formations; this is evidence
of reformation, not a runtime transform to copy.

The independent 27-unit move capture reaches median nearest-neighbor spacing
of about `0.334` tiles, with exact stacks and substantial overlap. Most units
travel near DAT speed, while lagging members temporarily use generic catch-up
factors near `1.09–1.20` and occasional `1.5`. This supports a future reusable
group-reformation controller with overlap and catch-up, rather than a fixed
per-matchup path.

Steppe/P4 forensics isolate the same access problem inside combat. P4's live
mean defeat clocks are `15.035 s` with 17 Steppe Lancers,
`12.656 s` with 24, and `12.843 s` with 23. The stable engine's corresponding
non-null means are `26.661 s`, `22.490 s`, and `22.327 s`, with two of the
17-Steppe seeds never defeating P4 at all. In one 17-Steppe replay, 16/17
units changed away from their first target; 12 changed while that target was
still alive. Their first acquisition named only two P4 bodies, but their first
attacks spread across five. Target-change delays have a strong mode near
`0.75 s`, consistent with an AI scan layered on top of route/formation state.

Two deliberately general target-side hypotheses were rejected. Allowing every
range-1 unit to attack any reachable auxiliary immediately flipped two Steppe
matchups. A one-time 0.8-second blocked-PATROL rescan kept winner direction but
worsened the three-row five-seed aggregate absolute delta from `83.93` to
`91.81` points. Neither is active. The evidence therefore points to the
movement/reformation state that makes those physical targets accessible, not
to a standalone retarget exception.

An initial half-tile compaction prototype was tested across the whole ranged
matrix and rejected. It produced three wrong winner directions, a `32.409`
mean absolute score delta, and only 2/14 rows within 20% winner HP. It is not
active. Its diagnostic report is retained under
`calibration/reports/ranged_matrix_candidate_one_seed_2026-08-29/` so that a
similar non-generalizing change is not repeated.

An isolated, direction-oriented collision-sized grid was also tested without
the other experimental targeting changes. It retained all winner directions
but regressed mean absolute score delta to `21.266` and reduced the strict HP
count to 4/14. That rules out static compact destinations alone. The missing
model needs dynamic slot reassignment/catch-up and must reproduce when units
leave formation on acquisition.
